How Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode Choose Their Sources

Google published a guide on how AI Overviews and AI Mode pick and link sources. Here’s what it says and how to make your pages eligible to be cited.

By the Zorvex Technologies team

On 24 November 2026 Google added a new Search Central page, Attribution and factuality in Search generative AI features. It explains how AI Overviews and AI Mode choose the pages they link to. Google says it describes how things already work rather than announcing a change, but it’s the clearest official explanation we’ve had.

1. It starts with normal search rankings

AI Overviews and AI Mode begin with the top search results and use their snippets as input for the AI answer. If your page doesn’t rank well in normal search, it’s much less likely to be cited in an AI answer. Rankings are still the entry ticket.

2. “Query fan-out” widens the net

The model may run extra, related searches to collect more material. A question like “best CRM for a small plumbing business” can spark sub-searches on pricing, integrations and reviews. Pages that answer these follow-up angles clearly get more chances to be pulled in.

3. Answers are checked against other sources

Google says cited pages are corroborated against top web results, data feeds and the Knowledge Graph. Claims that match what other trusted sources say are safer to cite. Accurate, consistent facts across your site, Google Business Profile and directories help.

4. Some topics are held back

Safety and policy checks can stop an AI answer from showing at all, especially for sensitive “Your Money or Your Life” topics or breaking news with little coverage.

5. You must be indexed and snippet-eligible

Only pages that are indexed and eligible to show a snippet in Search can appear as supporting links. If you block snippets with nosnippet or your page isn’t indexed, it can’t be cited.

6. Don’t fake freshness

Google says not to change a page’s date unless the content has substantially changed. Updating the date without real edits won’t help, so update the content itself.

What this means for your SEO

  • Keep doing the fundamentals. Technical health, indexing and strong rankings feed directly into AI citations.
  • Cover the follow-up questions. Add FAQs, comparisons and pricing details that match likely fan-out searches.
  • Write extractable answers. Put a clear, direct answer near the top of each section.
  • Keep facts consistent across your website, Google Business Profile and listings.
  • Check your snippet settings so you’re not accidentally opting out.

Google also says “optimising for generative AI search is optimising for the search experience, and thus still SEO.” We agree: AI visibility is built on good SEO, not a separate trick. See our SEO services.
