Google’s November 2026 Core Update and Gemini 4 Hydrogen: What Changed in Search

Google’s November 2026 core update has finished and Gemini 4 Hydrogen now powers AI Mode. Here’s what changed and what to check in Search Console.

By the Zorvex Technologies team

November was one of the busiest months for Google Search this year. A broad core update rolled out, AI Mode switched to a new model, and a Search Console bug made AI visibility look worse than it was for a few days. If your traffic moved this month, here’s what happened and how to read your numbers.

The November 2026 core update is complete

Google started rolling out the November 2026 core update on 10 November and said it could take up to two weeks. It finished on 24 November, after 14 days. Google has also said a December 2026 core update is coming, with a focus on complex queries and personalisation.

Core updates don’t target one type of site or one tactic. They change how Google weighs quality signals across the whole index, so some pages rise and others fall even if nothing on them changed.

What we recommend: wait at least a week after the rollout ends before you compare results. Google advises this, so a fair before-and-after comparison starts around 1 December. Compare a full week after the update with a full week before it started (ending 9 November), and look at pages and queries, not just total clicks.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen now runs AI Mode

On 12 November Google made Gemini 4 Hydrogen the default model in AI Mode for all users worldwide. Google says it’s also used for the hardest questions in AI Overviews, and Google’s own Search post called it the biggest model upgrade to Search yet.

This matters for anyone tracking AI visibility. If you measure how often AI Mode or AI Overviews cite your pages, your November data now spans two different models. A change in citations around 12 November may be the model, not your content.

A Search Console bug undercounted AI impressions

A logging error undercounted AI impressions in Search Console’s generative AI report from 9 to 11 November. Google says the error affected data logging only, so a dip on those days is missing data, not lost visibility. When a similar bug hit in August, Google restored the data a few days later, so these numbers may jump back up.

The bug overlaps the first day of the core update. Don’t read a sharp drop on 9–11 November as a penalty.

AI Overviews are getting more personal

On 23 November Google said AI Overviews and AI Mode will start tailoring some answers to a person’s recent searches. In practice, the answer your rank tracker records may not be the answer every searcher sees. Treat AI visibility tools as a sample, not a single “true” ranking.

Your checklist for December

  • Annotate 10 November (update start), 12 November (Hydrogen) and 24 November (update end) in your reports.
  • Exclude 9–11 November when you compare AI impressions in Search Console.
  • Compare winners and losers by page type (service pages, blog posts, location pages) to see what Google now rewards.
  • Improve pages that dropped with clearer answers, original data and expert input, rather than chasing quick fixes.
  • Expect more movement: a December core update is already announced.
